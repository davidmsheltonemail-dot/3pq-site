The dispute isn't born at the jobsite. It's born in the RFP — in the ambiguity that invites competing interpretations, the unstated assumptions that create implied warranties, and the procurement structure that selects for optimism instead of capability. By the time the first shovel hits dirt, the failure is already locked in. The contractor isn't the cause. The document is.

T H E   L E G A L   F O U N D A T I O N
United States v. Spearin, 248 U.S. 132 (1918)
"…if the contractor is bound to build according to plans and specifications prepared by the owner, the contractor will not be responsible for the consequences of defects in the plans and specifications."
The Spearin Doctrine holds that when an owner furnishes prescriptive specifications, they impliedly warrant the adequacy of those specifications. This implied warranty is not overcome by general clauses requiring the contractor to examine the site, check the plans, or assume responsibility until completion.

T H E   A N A L Y Z E R
3PQ Diagnostic™
Your procurement has structural defects. We find them.

Every procurement document makes structural commitments — who decides, who bears risk, what's validated before money changes hands, and whether the owner can stay out of the contractor's way. The Diagnostic tests those commitments against nine structural requirements. Violations predict specific failure modes. The analysis is surgical, citation-specific, and unflinching.

W H A T   I T   A N A L Y Z E S

Accountability Structure

Is authority singular or diffused? Does "shared risk" mean no one is accountable? Does "collaboration" mask the fact that the owner is co-authoring the design while the contractor bears all liability?

Sequencing Integrity

Is feasibility demonstrated before commitment? Is price fixed before value is competed? Or does the document commit to a price before anyone knows if the scope is achievable?

Owner Capacity

Can the owner refrain from intervention after signing? Does the structure require the owner to participate in solution development — breaking the accountability line?

Spearin Exposure

Every point where the owner furnishes design information creates an implied warranty. The Diagnostic maps every exposure point with predicted claim scenarios.

W H Y   T H I S   M A T T E R S   —   T H E   S P E A R I N   R I S K
Every prescriptive specification is an implied warranty. The Diagnostic finds every one.
Under United States v. Spearin (1918), when an owner furnishes plans and specifications, they impliedly warrant their adequacy. Every prescriptive detail — dimensions, materials, methods, products — creates an exposure point.
▸ Named products — warrant that the product will achieve the required performance
▸ Prescriptive assemblies — warrant that the assembly will perform as a system
▸ Dimensional requirements — warrant that the dimensions will accommodate the program
▸ Sequenced methods — warrant that the prescribed sequence is constructible

W H Y   T H I S   M A T T E R S   —   T H E   H I D D E N   P R O G R A M
The architect designed a building. But what did the Owner actually need?
Construction documents describe HOW a building is built. They rarely state WHY. The Owner's programmatic intent is buried in design decisions, equipment schedules, and room layouts. Project Harvest surfaces it.
▸ Three identical bays — the Owner needs modular, expandable workspace
▸ Rainwater to irrigation — water conservation is a program requirement
▸ Separate HVAC zones — environmental control is critical to program function
▸ 60% glazing ratio — natural daylight is essential, not aesthetic preference
T H E   B U I L D E R
3PQ RFP™
Built from what you need. Not what was drawn.

This is the document the Design-Builders see. Organized by Program (what the Owner needs) and Performance (how well it must work). Three-tier priority hierarchy creates genuine competition — Design-Builders compete on how much value they can deliver within a fixed price, not on who promises the most.

H O W   I T ' S   S T R U C T U R E D

Program Section

Project information, three-tier priority list, risk analysis, space criteria sheets with adjacency requirements, and functional intent. Every Design-Builder reads this and understands the complete problem.

Performance Section

Facility-level criteria (code, structural, energy, sustainability) and element-level performance by UniFormat division. States WHAT, never HOW. Products and manufacturers are never named.

Priority Hierarchy

Required — non-negotiable. Highly Desirable — compete on delivery. If Possible — stretch goals demonstrating superior capability. This IS competition on depth of value.

Substantiation Framework

Every requirement specifies what evidence the Design-Builder must submit and when — at proposal, preliminary design, design development, construction documents, and closeout.

W H Y   T H I S   M A T T E R S   —   C O M P E T I T I O N   O N   V A L U E
The lowest price doesn't win. The deepest value wins.
In a 3PQ RFP, price is fixed. Every Design-Builder proposes to the same budget. Competition shifts to how much of the priority list they can deliver — and how well they can substantiate their claims.
▸ Required tier — every proposer must meet these or they're non-responsive
▸ Highly Desirable tier — this is where competition lives
▸ If Possible tier — the differentiator that separates good from exceptional
